Dr. Yusra Abdulrahman, Assistant Professor of Aerospace Engineering and Deputy Director of the Advanced Research and Innovation Center (ARIC), Khalifa University

I build inspection systems that combine infrared thermography, robotics and AI, so aerospace manufacturers and maintenance teams can trust what they can't see.

My work sits between engineering and management: the sensing and algorithms that detect defects, and the industrial systems that put them to work on the factory floor and in the hangar. The same methods carry over to precision agriculture, where thermal and visual sensing guide decisions in the field.

In progress

From finding damage on the surface to seeing inside the structure.

How an inspection works

  1. Capture

    A robotic manipulator carries infrared and visual cameras across the part.

  2. Fuse

    Thermal, visual and process data are combined into one picture of the structure.

  3. Interpret

    AI models locate and classify defects such as impact damage and delamination.

  4. Trace

    Results are recorded in a trusted, traceable inspection record.

Phase I · running

Rapid detection of operational damage in composite structures

Multi-source data fusion for fast detection of in-service damage, sponsored by SAMC. A dedicated robotic thermography cell is installed at KU.

Phase II · approved, starting 2027

Seeing internal damage

Infrared thermography with robotic scanning to detect damage hidden below the surface of composite parts.

Three research directions with SAMC

Agreed with our industry partner as a mission-driven joint research model.

  • Integrated robotic intelligent inspection. Robots that combine visual and infrared inspection in one pass.
  • Hole-related defects and remaining life. Inspecting drilled and fastened areas and predicting how long they will last.
  • Trusted inspection data. Intelligent defect interpretation with blockchain-based traceability, so every result can be verified.
