AI, robotics and infrared thermography that find hidden damage in aerospace parts, and keep a record regulators can trust.

Dr. Yusra Abdulrahman, Assistant Professor of Aerospace Engineering and Deputy Director of the Advanced Research and Innovation Center (ARIC), Khalifa University

My group takes AI inspection out of the lab and into aircraft manufacturing and maintenance, from the camera to the robot to the certified record.

The work runs across four connected threads: AI-enabled visual inspection, non-destructive testing with infrared thermography, robotic and smart industrial systems, and blockchain-based traceability for inspection data. Partners include Sanad Aerotech, COMAC, SILAL and Beijing Institute of Technology, and the same methods now reach into precision agriculture.

Our publications are cited 92% above the world average for the field (SciVal, 2022–2025), and the 2023 review that set our roadmap ranks in the top 1% worldwide by field-weighted citation impact.

A roadmap, then the work

In 2023 we mapped what was stopping AI inspection from reaching the hangar. Since then, each project has tackled one of those problems.

Top 1%

of papers worldwide by field-weighted citation impact

Aero-engine blade defect detection: a systematic review of deep learning models

IEEE Access, 2023. A review of more than a hundred studies that organised the field by method, imaging modality and defect type, named the barriers to industrial use — scarce training data, the gap between simulation and reality, no shared benchmarks, and no deployed end-to-end systems — and set out the roadmap the group has followed since.

Problem: real, labelled images of damaged blades are almost impossible to collect at scale.

BladeSynth

A physics-based rendering pipeline that turns CAD blade models into realistic inspection images with synthetic defects and pixel-perfect labels. Models trained on it work on real blade images.

Scientific Data, 2025. Read paper ↗

Openpublic dataset and pipeline
Usedto train our deployed system

Problem: no published system covered the whole job, from picking up a blade to reporting its defects.

Robotic blade inspection

A robot finds each blade in a container, picks it up, images it, runs deep-learning defect detection and puts it back, built for maintenance-shop workflows.

Scientific Reports, 2026. Read paper ↗

~4 sper blade
88.2%mean average precision
1,290downloads in 3 months

Problem: thermography methods analyse each processed view of the heat signal on its own and miss what they show together.

PT-Fusion

A deep-learning model that fuses two standard pulse-thermography representations to find defects in composites and estimate how deep they are, with fewer parameters than leading transformer models.

Scientific Reports, 2026. Read paper ↗Code ↗

10%more accurate than U-Net-style baselines
Opencode and trained models

Problem: regulators need tamper-proof inspection records, but AI results are probabilistic and hard to audit.

BladeChain

A blockchain network shared by manufacturer, airline, maintenance shop and regulator. Every inspection records which AI model and version made the call, and new inspections trigger automatically on flight hours or cycles.

Scientific Reports, 2026. Read paper ↗

100%of lifecycle events captured

The first COMAC partnership in the Middle East

I built and coordinate Khalifa University's collaboration with COMAC through Shanghai Aircraft Manufacturing Co. (SAMC): KU's first aerospace partnership with China and COMAC's first in the Arab region.

  • A joint SAMC–KU NDT Joint Lab at Khalifa University, which I lead
  • A sponsored programme in rapid damage detection for composite aircraft structures, now running
  • Three research directions agreed with SAMC: robotic visual and infrared inspection, inspection and remaining-life prediction around drilled holes, and trusted inspection data
  • A competitive call I organised that opened funded COMAC projects to three more KU research teams
